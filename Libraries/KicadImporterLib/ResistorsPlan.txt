Resistors and RC filters in KiCad imports
==========================================

Planned 2026-11-26 (the user: "Plan out 1 and 2 please").  Nothing is built.  Pull-ups and pull-downs (a resistor
with one end on a supply) are a separate plan, still to write: they need a weak drive in MicroprocessorLib.


Why
---

LogiCim's KiCad importer leaves resistors and capacitors out, so a signal that goes through a resistor stops at it in
the simulation.  The user uses resistors two ways (2026-11-26): pull-ups and pull-downs, "to give an undriven trace a
value", and "as the R part of an RC filter to try and remove nanosecond or less glitches and try and smooth a
transition out slightly".  The decode check (CKicadSchemLogicimDecode) joins series resistors itself; this plan makes
every import do the right thing for the two kinds of series resistor the HCP65's boards have.

What the boards have (KiCad 10's netlists of the 135 schematics in HCP65/Kicad/Computer, 2026-11-26): about 458
resistor elements with neither end on a supply, on 30 boards, 248 pulls, 6 between supplies.

  1. Terminations: 196 elements of 220 ohm (CAY16-221J4LF) and 136 of 20 ohm (CAY16-200J4LF), with a few of 10,
     47 and 100, on buses, chip selects and clocks.  A capacitor is drawn beside only a few 20 and 47 ohm ones (5 to
     10pF: R x C 0.1 to 0.24ns).  The rest drive only what is on their net: a 74LVC input is about 4 to 5pF, a
     trace about 1pF for every 1 to 2cm, a connector a few pF.  So a 20 ohm termination is 0.1 to 0.6ns (a wire to
     any logic simulation), but a 220 ohm one is about 1.1ns into one input (a 10-90% rise of 2.4ns) and 4 to 7ns
     into a loaded bus line across a backplane (a rise of 10 to 15ns): as slow as a gate (74LVC at 3.3V: 1 to 5ns;
     74HCT: 12 to 22ns), so a real RC filter, which also swallows glitches of about a nanosecond.  What a
     termination removes besides is ringing on a fast edge, which no logic simulation models.  Also with no supply
     at either end: LED current limiters (the LEDs aren't simulated), the PS/2 lines' 150 ohm resistors, the Audio
     DAC's op-amp resistors (the op-amp is a placeholder).
  2. Slow: RCs that are the circuit's function, R x C of nanoseconds to milliseconds.
       Clock Doubler: Fast CLK -> 74LVC1G17 (Schmitt buffer) -> Clean Fast CLK -> R1 750 ohm -> C1 16pF (12ns) ->
         MAX9010 comparator IN-, against IN+ on the 1.6V net (R2/R3 4.7k divider from 3.3V, a supply by its label
         since 2026-11-26) -> OUT -> 74LVC2G86 XOR with Clean Fast CLK -> 2x Fast CLK: a pulse on every edge.
       PS2 Timing: ~{Clock} -> R3 4.7k, and D1's diode across it -> RC Packet Pulse, C3 0.1uF (0.47ms) -> a
         74VHCT14 Schmitt input; Packet Pulse -> R1 || R2 (10k each, 5k) and D1's other diode -> RC Code Pulse,
         C4 1uF (5ms) -> another 74VHCT14 input.  The diode charges the capacitor at once one way and the resistor
         lets it go slowly the other: a retriggerable pulse stretcher, which says when a burst of PS/2 clocks ends.

How the simulation already works (MicroprocessorLib, Docs/libraries/microprocessorlib/logic.md): a trace is analog,
an output slews its trace from one voltage to another over its family's slew time, and every input on the trace
schedules a transition for when the voltage crosses its threshold (high at or above VIH, low at or below VIL,
unsettled between, which is ignored).  Time is 1/1024ths of a nanosecond.


Part 1: fast series resistors become wires (KicadImporterLib)
----------------------------------------------------------

1. Values.  A reader of a resistor's and a capacitor's value from its symbol: "22Ω", "10KΩ", "4.7KΩ", "16pF", "1μF",
   "0.1µF" (both micro signs), and the networks' part codes, CAY16-200J4LF and 4609X-101-103LF (two digits and a
   power of ten).  Tested on every R, RN and C value in HCP65/Kicad.  And each family's input capacitance (CI),
   from its datasheets, a column added to PropagationTimes.csv (LogiCim's Resources and the tests' Input copies,
   CommonLibraries' TestMicroprocessorLib's too) and read with the family's other figures.
2. Resistor elements.  An R is one element; a network's elements are its pins paired by pin name, as
   CKicadSchemLogicimDecode::JoinSeriesResistors pairs them (move that pairing into a shared function).  A bussed
   network (one common pin) has no element whose ends are two signals: its common pin is never joined.
3. The rule, in CKicadSchemLogicimConverter: an element whose two nets aren't supplies (IsKicadSchemPowerNetName, or
   a supply label) joins them into one net, unless it makes a slow RC: R x C of 1ns or more, C being the capacitors
   from either of its nets to a supply or, where none is drawn, an estimate of what it drives (each input on the
   driven net at its family's input capacitance, from the datasheets, plus a few pF for the wiring; see question
   6), and those are part 2's.  A joined net is drawn as one net: its labels' tunnels share a name
   (the decode's way); the resistor stays out of the drawing, as now.
4. Warnings, in the import's report: a series resistor whose value can't be read is joined; a slow RC is left apart
   until part 2 is built, so the circuit is the same as today there.
5. The decode uses the converter's joins: JoinSeriesResistors goes, and LogiCim --decode prints what it prints now.
6. Tests: a sheet with an R, an isolated network, a bussed network, a fast RC (22 ohm, 10pF: joined) and a slow one
   (1k, 1nF: apart, warned); the expected circuits regenerated and their differences checked (only nets joined);
   TestKicadImporterLib; the website's decode check (22 rows, no differences).


Part 2: slow RCs become delay elements (MicroprocessorLib, LogicimLib, KicadImporterLib)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7. An exponential slew (MicroprocessorLib).  Today a slew is a straight line from one voltage to another.  An RC's
   output is the capacitor's curve: V(t) = Vend + (Vstart - Vend) e^(-t/RC), which never quite arrives.  A slew
   shape beside the linear one: CSlewEvent's voltage at a time, and the time an input's threshold is crossed,
   worked out from the curve (t = RC ln((Vstart - Vend) / (Vthreshold - Vend))); "arrived" when within the family's
   noise of Vend.  A new slew started before an old one crossed an input's threshold drops that input's pending
   transition: check this is already so for linear slews (a short pulse then changes nothing: the glitch filtering
   falls out of it), and test it.
8. An RC element (MicroprocessorLib): a part with an input port and an output port, R x C its time constant for a
   rise and for a fall (a diode across the resistor makes one of them nearly zero: the charge through the diode).
   Its input reading the driving trace's level, it slews its output trace along the curve toward that level from
   wherever the output is now.  An undriven input: the output keeps its voltage (the capacitor holds it).
9. An analog comparator (MicroprocessorLib): output high while V(IN+) > V(IN-), after its propagation delay (the
   MAX9010's, from its datasheet).  It reads its inputs' voltages, not their levels; a transition when the slewing
   input crosses the other's voltage, by the same sum as step 7.  To begin with one input must be a fixed voltage
   (a supply, as the Clock Doubler's IN+ is); both moving is left until a board needs it.
10. Schmitt inputs.  The 74x14 and 74x1G17 have hysteresis: rising, they switch at VT+; falling, at VT-.  An input's
    unsettled band (between VIL and VIH, ignored) already behaves this way: check it on a slow slew, and if it does,
    a Schmitt part's family uses its datasheet's VT+ as VIH and VT- as VIL (HCP65/Datasheets has the 74VHCT14's and
    the 74LVC1G17's).  If it doesn't, a Schmitt flag on the input.
11. LogicimLib: views for the RC element (drawn as a resistor feeding a capacitor to ground, its R, C and diode
    shown) and the comparator (a triangle with + and -), saved and loaded in a circuit's XML and in a simulation's
    state, as every other component is.
12. KicadImporterLib: a slow RC (part 1, step 3) becomes an RC element between the two nets, the capacitor's
    supply its ground; a diode across the resistor (anode on one net, cathode on the other) sets which way is fast.
    A comparator symbol (IN+, IN-, OUT; the MAX9010) becomes a comparator.
13. Tests.  MicroprocessorLib: crossing times worked out by hand for a rise and a fall; a pulse shorter than the
    crossing time changing nothing; the diode's fast direction; the comparator against a fixed voltage.  LogiCim:
    the Clock Doubler imported and run with a clock on Fast CLK gives a pulse on 2x Fast CLK at every edge, as wide
    as R1 x C1 to the comparator's threshold plus the parts' delays; the PS2 Timing with a burst of clocks on Clock
    holds RC Packet Pulse high through the burst and lets it fall about R3 x C3 x ln(5/VT-) after the last.
    TestMicroprocessorLib, TestLogicimLib, TestKicadImporterLib; the decode check.


Questions for the user (put one at a time, recommendation first)
------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Where fast ends and slow begins: recommended 1ns (R x C), about the fastest gate delay on the boards.  The 20
     ohm terminations (0.1 to 0.6ns) fall below it and the Clock Doubler (12ns) above; the 220 ohm ones sit at it
     (1.1ns into one input) and go over it as they drive more, so question 6 decides them.
     Decided 2026-11-26: 1ns (the user leaves the electronics to my recommendation: "whatever you suggest goes").
  2. A joined net's name: recommended the name a label gives it (the connector's side if both have labels), else
     the driven side's.
     Decided 2026-11-26 (the user: "The name a label gives it"): as recommended.
  3. Part 2's order: recommended the simulation first (steps 7 to 10, with MicroprocessorLib's tests), then the
     views and the importer.
     Decided 2026-11-26 (the user): as recommended.
  4. How an RC element and a comparator look in LogiCim: recommended as above (step 11).
  5. Whether a MAX9010-like part is "a comparator" by its symbol's pin names (IN+, IN-, OUT) or by a list of parts.
  6. Where no capacitor is drawn, the capacitance a termination drives: recommended estimated, each input on the net
     at its family's input capacitance (a column added to PropagationTimes.csv from the datasheets: about 4pF for
     74LVC) plus a fixed few pF for the wiring (one figure to agree), so the 220 ohm terminations become RC elements
     (about 1ns and up) and the 20 ohm ones stay wires.  The alternative, joining every termination with no drawn
     capacitor as a wire, gets the logic right but each 220 ohm one's timing a few ns early.
     Decided 2026-11-26 (the user: "Estimate it"): estimated.  The wiring, by my recommendation (the user: "whatever
     you suggest goes"): 3pF a net (a few cm of trace and the pads), and 10pF more for each connector pin on it,
     standing for the backplane and the boards beyond it that a one-board import can't see; both named constants,
     to change when boards are simulated together.


Order
-----

Part 1 first: it is small, needs no new simulation, and every import's buses and chip selects then reach where they
go.  Part 2 after, its MicroprocessorLib steps before the views and the importer.  Pull-ups and pull-downs are the
next plan.
