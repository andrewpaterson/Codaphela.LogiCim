Pull-ups and pull-downs in KiCad imports
=========================================

Planned 2026-11-26 (the user: "A weak drive sounds good").  Built (below).  Built after ResistorsPlan.txt's part
2: a released line rises along its RC curve (step 7 there) and its capacitance is that plan's estimate (question 6
there).  The electronics choices here are mine (the user, 2026-11-26: "whatever you suggest goes").


Why
---

The user's pulls are "to give an undriven trace a value" (2026-11-26).  KiCad 10's netlists of the 135 schematics in
HCP65/Kicad/Computer (2026-11-26) have about 330 pull elements (a resistor, or a network's element, with one end on
a supply): 10k about 190, 6.8k about 75, 4.7k 18, 3.3k 17, 33k 12, 1k 8, and a few others.  They are:

  1. Lines held at a value when nothing drives them, most of them: address and data buses behind a buffer that is
     off (Address Buffer 16bit's A0 to A15), active low controls (~{RD}, ~{WD}, Main Memory Full Enable's ~{Main}0
     to ~{Main}3), each device's interrupt line, pulled high so an empty socket reads no interrupt (the Processor
     Board's 4609X networks), and lines that only go to a connector.
  2. Switch inputs: the pull is the line's value with the switch open (Clock Hold Select's S1).  LogiCim has no
     switch part, so the line keeps that value.
  3. Outside the logic: LED resistors and the Audio DAC's bias dividers and 1M resistors.  Left out, as now.

Today the importer leaves them out, so a released bus or an empty socket's line is undriven: unknown to every input
on it (logic.md's decision: undriven inputs are unknown only).  Drawn as ordinary drivers they would be wrong: a
trace's voltage is the average of what drives it (CTrace::GetVoltage), so a 10k pull-up against an output driving
low would read 1.65V at 3.3V, where on the board the output's 25 ohm or so wins and the line reads about 0.01V.


The model (decided)
-------------------

A pull is a weak drive: a supply's voltage through a resistance.

  - Strong drivers win: a trace's voltage is the average of its strong drivers (as now), and the weak ones count
    only when no strong one drives it.  Not a resistance on every output with a conductance weighted average over
    them all: more exact, but it changes every trace's voltage to fix what only the pulls need.
  - Two or more weak drives on an undriven trace share by conductance, a divider: V = sum(Vi / Ri) / sum(1 / Ri).
  - Released, a trace goes to its weak voltage along the RC curve, R the weak drives' resistance in parallel, C the
    trace's capacitance: 10k into 10pF is 100ns (a 10-90% rise of 220ns), longer on the backplane.  A read of an
    empty socket's line doesn't settle at once, as on the board.
  - Undriven stays unknown: a pulled trace is never undriven.


Power
-----

The user (2026-11-26): "the way power works in Logicim is a bit weird / broken.  Power is instantaneously applied
on connection."  A supply (CPowerOutPort) drives its fixed voltage the moment it is connected, a chip asks
IsPowered when it acts, and nothing ramps VCC up or tells a chip its power came or went.  This plan doesn't fix
that and doesn't lean on it: a pull reads its supply as a chip does, and a supply connected or cut while the
simulation runs moves a pull's level as suddenly as it moves a chip's.  A power-up model (VCC ramping, parts told
when their power arrives) would be its own plan; the pulls would then follow their supply along their RC curve.

Steps
-----

MicroprocessorLib (with logic.md's section on traces and ports, and its decisions)

1. A weak port: CPullPort, a CPort with a resistance, driving its pulled voltage weakly.  CPort says whether a port's
   drive is weak (a virtual beside GetVoltageOut; a strong port's resistance is none).  A pull part (a CPortHolder:
   a CPowerInPort on its supply, a CPullPort on its signal) pulls to its supply's voltage, and to nothing while the
   supply has none.  It treats power as the chips do today (IsPowered at the time asked), and no better: see
   Power below.
2. CTrace::GetVoltage (and GetMinimumVoltage, GetMaximumVoltage, GetShortVoltage): the strong drivers' average; with
   none, the weak drivers' conductance weighted average; with neither, NoVoltage.  A trace's capacitance, set by
   whoever builds the circuit (the importer's estimate; a default for one drawn by hand, the same 3pF a net).
3. Releasing.  When the last strong driver stops (a drive of NaN, WriteImpedance, Disconnect: the paths that today
   reach CTrace::NotifyUndriven), a trace with weak drivers isn't undriven: it slews from the voltage it had along
   ResistorsPlan's exponential curve to the weak voltage, and its inputs transition when it crosses their
   thresholds, as for any slew.  A strong driver starting on a weakly held trace slews from the trace's voltage, not
   from undriven (check what CSlewEvent::Update does there today).
4. Start: a pull on a powered supply drives from time 0, so a line nothing else drives starts at its pulled level,
   not unknown, as a chip drives what it remembers from the start (CIntegratedCircuit::WriteOutputs).

LogicimLib

5. A pull component: a resistor to a supply symbol (up) or a ground symbol (down), its resistance shown; saved and
   loaded in a circuit's XML and a simulation's state, as every other component is.  A trace held only weakly is
   coloured as a weak level (lighter than a driven one) where traces are coloured by voltage.

KicadImporterLib

6. A resistor element (ResistorsPlan step 2's) with one end on a supply (IsKicadSchemPowerNetName, or a supply
   label) and the other on a net that has a logic port or a connector pin becomes a pull on that net.  One on a net
   with neither (an LED's, an analog part's) is left out, as now, and counted in the import's report.  A value that
   can't be read is a 10k pull, warned.
7. The net's capacitance is ResistorsPlan's estimate (its inputs, 3pF a net, 10pF a connector pin), the same
   function.

Tests

8. MicroprocessorLib: a strong output against a 10k pull reads the output's voltage; a released trace crosses VIH at
   R x C x ln(...) worked out by hand; two pulls divide; a strong driver taking a weakly held trace; a pull with an
   unpowered supply.  LogiCim: Address Buffer 16bit imported, its buffer off, reads A0 to A15 at the pulled level
   once the release slew ends; the Processor Board with an empty device socket reads its interrupt line high.
   The expected circuits of the boards with pulls regenerated and their differences checked (only pulls added);
   TestMicroprocessorLib, TestLogicimLib, TestKicadImporterLib; the website's decode check.
